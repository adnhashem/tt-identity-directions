Information

Read

A weak signal is a small piece of news that few people notice today and that may be the first sign of a major change tomorrow: a pilot in a distant city, a patent filing, a complaint that keeps coming up among a small group of customers. On its own it proves nothing. Its value is that it arrives before the numbers do.

A trend is something different. A trend is a change with a direction you can measure over years, such as rising life expectancy or shopping moving to the phone. When similar signals keep appearing from sources that have nothing to do with one another, a trend is probably forming.

When we scan, we cover five areas: society, technology, the economy, the environment, and politics and regulation. For each signal we ask three questions. Where did it come from, and can the source be trusted? What would change in our sector if it grew? What should we watch to know that it is growing? The most common mistake is to collect only the signals that confirm what we already believe.
